# NATURE Trial: speaker notes

## 1. NATURE Trial

- Good morning. Today's journal club is the NATURE trial, presented as a late-breaking trial at ESC Congress 2026 in Munich.
- It asks one question: does pulling out a large clot with a stent-retriever, before stenting, reduce heart damage in STEMI?

## 2. Why a large thrombus matters

- A big clot is a problem during primary PCI.
- Balloon and stent can break it and push debris downstream: distal embolisation.
- That blocks the microcirculation, so the artery opens but the muscle does not reperfuse, and the infarct is larger.
- The idea is to remove the clot first and break this chain.

## 3. What we already know

- We have tried this before with manual aspiration.
- TASTE, 7,244 patients: no mortality benefit.
- TOTAL, over 10,000 patients: no clinical benefit and more strokes at 30 days.
- So ESC guidelines say routine aspiration is not recommended, Class III.

## 4. TAPAS vs TASTE vs TOTAL vs NATURE

- Putting the trials side by side.
- TAPAS was positive on a surrogate, myocardial blush; TASTE and TOTAL then showed no hard-outcome benefit, and TOTAL had a stroke signal.
- NATURE differs in two ways: only patients with a large thrombus, and a retriever instead of aspiration.
- But like TAPAS, its positive result is on a surrogate endpoint. Keep that lesson in mind.

## 5. Trial design

- Prospective, multicentre, randomised trial: 154 STEMI patients with large thrombus at 11 centres.
- Randomised 1:1 to enVast thrombectomy then PCI, or standard PCI.
- Hypothesis: enVast is superior on infarct size. PI Prof. Marco Valgimigli, Lugano; sponsored by Vesalio.

## 6. Inclusion criteria

- Adults with STEMI: chest pain over 20 minutes with ST elevation, or the posterior MI equivalent.
- Treated within 8 hours of symptom onset.
- The key criterion: TIMI thrombus grade 3 or more, re-checked after wiring if the artery is occluded.

## 7. TIMI thrombus grading

- A quick reminder of the grading.
- Grade 3 is a definite clot more than half the vessel diameter; grade 4 is two vessel diameters or more; grade 5 is total occlusion.
- NATURE took grade 3 and above, and grade 5 had to be re-graded once flow was restored.

## 8. Exclusion criteria

- Excluded: unconscious patients, small vessels under 2.5 mm, heavy calcification or tortuosity, stent thrombosis, prior MI in the same territory, women of child-bearing potential, and other trials.
- Mostly anatomy where a retriever is hard to deliver, or things that would confuse infarct size.

## 9. Endpoints

- Primary: infarct size by CK-MB area under the curve, a blood-test measure.
- Secondary: infarct size on cardiac MRI at day 3.
- Safety: death, stroke and MACE at 30 days.

## 10. Primary endpoint: met

- The primary endpoint was met.
- Infarct size by CK-MB was 26% smaller with enVast, P = 0.001.
- The chart shows relative values, with standard PCI set to 100.

## 11. Secondary endpoint: cardiac MRI

- On cardiac MRI at day 3, infarct size was about 25% smaller with enVast.
- This was a trend, not formally significant, but it points the same way as the CK-MB result.

## 12. Safety at 30 days

- No strokes and no deaths in the enVast arm.
- Stroke 0 vs 1.3%, death 0 vs 2.6%, MACE 1.3 vs 3.8%.
- Reassuring after the stroke signal in TOTAL, but these are very few events and the trial was not powered for them.

## 14. Strengths

- Randomised and multicentre.
- The right population: only large thrombus, where benefit is most plausible.
- Two different measures agree, and there was no early safety signal.

## 15. Limitations

- Small, only 154 patients.
- Surrogate endpoint, not death or heart failure.
- Operators could not be blinded, the MRI result was only a trend, and the trial was industry-funded.

## 16. Conclusion

- In STEMI with a large thrombus, enVast before PCI reduced infarct size compared with standard PCI, with no early safety concern.
- Today it is promising for selected cases but not routine; guidelines are unchanged.
- Next we need a larger trial with hard outcomes.

## 17. For our cath lab

- Three practical points for us.
- Grade the thrombus in every STEMI.
- Routine manual aspiration is still not recommended.
- Stent-retriever thrombectomy is promising for large thrombus, but we wait for outcome data.
