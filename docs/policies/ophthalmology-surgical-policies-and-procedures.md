## 1. Purpose

To establish the requirement that all surgery-related policies and procedures governing
ophthalmic surgical care are **written, approved, accessible, current, and auditable**, and to
define the minimum set of topics that must be covered. Documented policy is the mechanism by
which the organization makes ophthalmic surgical practice consistent across surgeons, sites, and
shifts; supports the prevention of never events such as wrong-site, wrong-patient, wrong-implant
surgery; and demonstrates compliance to regulators, accreditors, and payers.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to every surgical or invasive ophthalmic procedure performed under the
organization's authority, regardless of setting or anesthesia type, including but not limited to:

- Cataract extraction with or without intraocular lens (IOL) implantation
- Corneal procedures (keratoplasty, DSAEK/DMEK, cross-linking, pterygium excision)
- Glaucoma surgery (trabeculectomy, tube shunt, MIGS)
- Vitreoretinal surgery (vitrectomy, scleral buckle, retinal detachment repair)
- Intravitreal injections and other office-based invasive procedures
- Oculoplastic and orbital surgery (ptosis repair, blepharoplasty, dacryocystorhinostomy, enucleation, evisceration)
- Strabismus surgery
- Refractive surgery (LASIK, PRK, phakic IOL)
- Laser procedures (YAG capsulotomy, laser peripheral iridotomy, panretinal photocoagulation, SLT)

It applies to all personnel involved in those procedures: attending ophthalmologists, fellows,
residents, anesthesia professionals, registered nurses, surgical technologists, ophthalmic
technicians, sterile processing staff, and contracted or locum personnel.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Procedural document** | A written policy, procedure, protocol, standard work instruction, checklist, or preference card that directs clinical or operational practice. |
| **Operative eye** | The eye designated for surgery, documented as "right eye (OD)" or "left eye (OS)" in words, never by abbreviation alone in the consent. |
| **Universal Protocol** | The pre-procedure verification, site marking, and time-out process required to prevent wrong-site, wrong-procedure, and wrong-person surgery. |
| **Implant record** | The permanent record of any device placed in or on the eye, including IOLs, glaucoma drainage devices, scleral buckles, punctal plugs, and orbital implants. |
| **TASS** | Toxic Anterior Segment Syndrome — acute sterile postoperative inflammation, typically caused by contaminants on instruments or in intraocular solutions. |
| **Controlled document** | A document under version control, with an owner, approval record, effective date, and scheduled review date. |

## 4. Policy statements

### 4.1 Documentation is mandatory
Every surgery-related practice that affects patient safety, clinical outcome, regulatory
compliance, or billing integrity in ophthalmology **shall** be captured in a controlled document.
Undocumented custom or individual surgeon preference is not a substitute for policy. Where a
surgeon's technique varies legitimately from the standard, that variation shall be recorded on an
approved surgeon preference card, which is itself a controlled document.

### 4.2 Minimum required topic set
The Ophthalmic Surgical Services Director shall maintain documented policies and procedures
covering, at minimum, every topic listed in [Section 6](#6-required-policy-and-procedure-inventory).
An annual gap analysis against that inventory is required.

### 4.3 Control and accessibility
Each procedural document shall carry a document number, version, owner, approval body, approval
date, effective date, and next review date. The current approved version shall be accessible to
all staff at the point of care — electronically at every ophthalmic OR workstation, and in a
readable offline copy available during network or power failure. Superseded versions shall be
withdrawn from circulation and retained in an archive per the organization's record-retention
schedule.

### 4.4 Review and revision
Documents shall be reviewed at least annually. Review shall be triggered ahead of schedule by any
of: a relevant adverse event or near miss; a change in law, regulation, or accreditation
standard; introduction of a new procedure, device, implant, or drug; a change in physical site or
scope of service; or an internal or external audit finding.

### 4.5 Education and attestation
Staff shall be educated on new and materially revised documents before the effective date.
Attestation of review shall be recorded for each person whose practice the document governs, and
retained in the training record. Onboarding of new personnel, including locums and contracted
staff, shall include the ophthalmic surgical document set relevant to their role.

### 4.6 Auditability
Compliance with documented procedures shall be verified through scheduled audit — at minimum
quarterly observational audit of the Universal Protocol and time-out, and quarterly audit of
sterilization and implant records. Audit findings, corrective actions, and re-audit results shall
be reported to the Surgical Services Committee.

## 7. Procedure — maintaining the documented set

1. **Inventory.** The Ophthalmic Surgical Services Director maintains a register of all controlled
   documents in scope, listing document number, title, owner, version, approval date, effective
   date, and next review date.
2. **Gap analysis.** At least annually, the register is compared against
   [Section 6](#6-required-policy-and-procedure-inventory). Every topic without a current approved
   document is logged as a gap with a named owner and a due date.
3. **Drafting and revision.** The content owner drafts or revises the document. Any document
   touching sterilization, medication, or anesthesia is routed to Infection Prevention, Pharmacy,
   or Anesthesia respectively for concurrence before approval.
4. **Approval.** Clinical documents are approved by the Medical Executive Committee; operational
   documents by the Surgical Services Committee. Approval is recorded with date and approving body.
5. **Publication.** The approved version is published to the point-of-care repository, the prior
   version is withdrawn and archived, and the register is updated.
6. **Education.** Staff affected by a new or materially revised document are educated before the
   effective date, and attestations are recorded.
7. **Audit.** Compliance is audited on the schedule in [Section 4.6](#46-auditability). Findings go
   to the Surgical Services Committee with corrective actions and re-audit dates.
8. **Escalation.** A document more than 30 days past its review date is reported to the Chief of
   Ophthalmology; more than 90 days past, to the Medical Executive Committee.

## 8. Compliance and enforcement

Failure to follow an approved surgical policy or procedure is managed under the organization's
professional conduct and corrective action processes. Repeated non-compliance with verification
procedures — particularly the Universal Protocol, implant verification, or count procedures — is
reviewed through the professional practice evaluation process and may affect privileges. Reporting
a near miss or an error in good faith is protected and shall not itself be a basis for corrective
action.

## Appendix B — Ophthalmic time-out minimum content

The time-out is performed immediately before incision, with all team members present, active, and
free of competing tasks. Verbal confirmation is required for each item:

1. Patient identity, confirmed with two identifiers
2. Procedure to be performed, stated in full
3. **Operative eye**, stated as right or left, confirmed against the consent, the site mark, and
   the source document — not against the schedule alone
4. Implant type, model, and power, confirmed against the biometry source document
5. Allergies
6. Anesthesia plan and any block already performed, including the eye blocked
7. Antibiotic prophylaxis given or intentionally omitted
8. Anticipated complications, special equipment, and backup device availability
9. Sterilization indicator verification for the instrument set in use
10. Explicit confirmation that all team members agree, and that anyone may stop the procedure if
    a discrepancy exists

Any unresolved discrepancy halts the procedure until the source document is retrieved and the
discrepancy is resolved and documented.
